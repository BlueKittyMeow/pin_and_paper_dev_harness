# Multi-select & Grouping UX Research for Pin & Paper

## Scope and confidence
I used official docs/support pages where possible, plus a small number of reviews/tutorial transcripts where the official docs were thin. Zinnia is the least formally documented of the set, so some Zinnia details are evidence-backed but not fully verified in Pixite’s own docs.

## 1. Zinnia: what I could verify

Zinnia clearly supports single-object selection plus a long-hold lasso for multi-select. Pixite’s support article on sticker creation says to “select all of it” with a “long hold for the lasso,” then use the bottom toolbar to group the items; the same article says a selected result can be saved as a reusable sticker/favorite ([Pixite support](https://support.pixiteapps.com/hc/en-us/articles/32297292994067-Create-Stickers-in-Zinnia)). Pixite’s template-editing article also shows tap-selection of a placed template/object, then repeated ungrouping to drill into nested groups, which implies grouped content is selected as a unit first and then progressively unpacked ([Pixite support](https://support.pixiteapps.com/hc/en-us/articles/32296586295059-Editing-Templates-by-Grouping-or-Ungrouping-Objects)).

The actions available on a selection are broader than Pixite’s help center spells out. The App Store listing explicitly says Zinnia lets you resize and move objects, send them to back/front, lock a layer in place, crop, rotate, and more; reviews also describe a selection toolbar with copy, paste, crop, flip, and color-change actions ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/zinnia-digital-planner/id1485310935), [Paperless X review](https://beingpaperless.com/zinnia-planner-journal-for-ipad-complete-review-2023/)). A tutorial transcript also describes grouping individual letter stickers by holding the Apple Pencil until the lasso appears, then grouping and resizing the result ([tutorial transcript mirror](https://grljournals.in/new/blog.php/?blog_id=im-hooked-brand-new-journal-and-planner-app--zinnia-review)).

On draw-vs-select interaction, the evidence points to a tool/mode model, but I could not fully verify the current rules from Pixite docs. The tutorial transcript shows drawing tools and text tools being entered from the top toolbar, then exited with “Done,” while selection is invoked by long-hold lasso or by tapping an object ([tutorial transcript mirror](https://grljournals.in/new/blog.php/?blog_id=im-hooked-brand-new-journal-and-planner-app--zinnia-review)). A French tutorial also describes returning to a read-only state after using draw/text tools and says selected objects expose options along the bottom ([Le Buro de Lola](https://leburodelola.fr/tuto-zinnia/)). I could not verify a documented rectangle marquee, documented add/remove-from-selection semantics, or a current official explanation of how Apple Pencil selection differs from finger touch in the editor.

My read: Zinnia’s model is object-first, lasso-heavy, and explicitly layered. It seems optimized for “compose, lasso, group, move, restyle, reorder” rather than for desktop-style modifier selection. The biggest weakness called out by reviewers is poor selectivity once a page gets dense ([Paperless X review](https://beingpaperless.com/zinnia-planner-journal-for-ipad-complete-review-2023/)).

## 2. Comparative survey

**Apple Freeform.** Freeform uses several touch-friendly multi-select paths: hold one item and tap others with another finger, hold empty canvas and drag a box, or enter a “Select Objects” state and tap items to add/remove them. Grouping is explicit from an Arrange menu, and z-order is explicit too: Back/Front on iPad, and Bring Forward/Bring to Front / Send Backward / Send to Back on Mac. Apple also exposes a dedicated Apple Pencil “Select and Scroll” toggle, which is unusually explicit about draw-vs-select mode switching ([iPad guide](https://support.apple.com/en-nz/guide/ipad/ipad0b4f22b0/ipados), [Mac arrange guide](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/guide/freeform/frfm6cb2dcdd/mac), [Mac shortcuts](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/freeform/frfm46c25187e/mac)).

**Milanote.** Milanote behaves less like a vector editor and more like a spatial board organizer. Official docs clearly show long-press-canvas drag for multi-select on iPad, space-drag for panning on desktop, and explicit send-forward/send-back shortcuts. But its main “grouping” mental model is not arbitrary object grouping; it is grouping selected items into a Column container. I did not find official docs for a generic group/ungroup object model comparable to Figma or Concepts ([keyboard shortcuts](https://help.milanote.com/en/articles/111393-keyboard-shortcuts), [Columns](https://help.milanote.com/en/articles/10478526-columns), [lock position](https://help.milanote.com/en/articles/4357447-locking-an-item-to-your-board)).

**Concepts.** Concepts has the richest touch/stylus selection model in this set. Selection is a first-class tool, but it can also be invoked by tap-and-hold; it offers lasso, item picker, and color picker, plus filters for partial stroke selection, locked content, and layers. It explicitly supports additive/subtractive selection, grouping, locking, duplication, and transform gestures. It also cleanly separates inputs: finger action can be configured as Pan Canvas or Select while stylus continues drawing, and two fingers are the standard pan/zoom/rotate gesture. Notably, Concepts says the active selection is visually brought to front, but that reads as a temporary interaction aid, not a persistent z-order edit ([Concepts manual: Selection](https://concepts.app/en/manual/selection), [Settings](https://concepts.app/en/manual/settings), [Getting Started](https://concepts.app/en/manual)).

**Goodnotes.** Goodnotes uses an explicit Lasso tool rather than implicit selection. Its lasso supports rectangle by default and freehand if you switch modes; once selected, the object menu exposes cut/copy/duplicate/delete, resize/rotate, color, screenshot, add element, image crop, handwriting conversion, and explicit front/back ordering. Goodnotes also has a hard edit/navigation split through Read Only mode versus Edit mode. I did not find evidence of a strong persistent group/ungroup model for arbitrary mixed objects; the core mental model is “temporary lasso selection” rather than “create groups and manage them as objects” ([Lasso tool](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/10779390143247-Select-content-with-the-Lasso-Tool), [Working with Objects](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/14141163656335-Working-with-Objects), [Read Only Mode](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/7452151771663-Read-Only-Mode)).

**Figma / FigJam.** Figma on desktop is the clearest reference for mouse/keyboard semantics: click to select, `Shift`-click to add/remove, drag on empty canvas for marquee, `Cmd/Ctrl` for deep selection of nested layers, group/frame/component from selection, and explicit z-order through both shortcuts and the layers panel. Panning is separated with spacebar/hand tool. FigJam inherits some of that on desktop, but its iPad behavior is more touch-specific: selection only works when drawing tools are inactive; multi-select is long-press-drag, long-press then “Select objects,” or a precision-select circle tool; grouping and locking are explicit actions. It also supports two-finger panning while drawing, which is a very strong pattern for whiteboard apps ([Figma selection](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040449873-Select-layers-and-objects), [Figma arrange](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039956914-Adjust-alignment-rotation-and-position), [Figma navigation](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/30925881896727-FD4B-Navigate-Figma-Design-files), [FigJam iPad](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4502073572247-FigJam-for-iPad), [FigJam grouping](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500004414962-Group-objects-in-FigJam), [FigJam pan/zoom](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/1500004414582-Pan-and-zoom-in-FigJam)).

## 3. Touch vs desktop: what translates, what does not

Desktop apps converge on the same core pattern: single-click selection, `Shift`-click add/remove, marquee from empty canvas, and a separate pan gesture via spacebar/hand tool. That pattern is precise, low-friction, and scales well to dense canvases. Figma is the cleanest example; Milanote also follows the “selection tool plus space-to-pan” family ([Figma](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040449873-Select-layers-and-objects), [Milanote](https://help.milanote.com/en/articles/111393-keyboard-shortcuts)).

Touch apps diverge because finger input makes “drag to select” collide with “drag to pan.” The better touch apps resolve this in one of three ways: explicit mode/button switching (Freeform Apple Pencil Select and Scroll, Goodnotes Read Only/Edit), long-press to begin selection (Freeform, Milanote, FigJam), or input-role separation where stylus draws and fingers pan/select (Concepts, FigJam while drawing) ([Freeform](https://support.apple.com/en-nz/guide/ipad/ipad0b4f22b0/ipados), [Goodnotes](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/7452151771663-Read-Only-Mode), [Concepts](https://concepts.app/en/manual/settings), [FigJam](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4502073572247-FigJam-for-iPad)).

The strongest cross-input pattern is not “make touch work like desktop.” It is “keep the mental model consistent, but let the gesture differ.” Multi-select should still mean “collect a set, then act on it,” but desktop can use modifiers and marquee while touch should use long-press, explicit select state, or stylus/finger role separation.

## 4. Selection vs canvas gestures: how good apps disambiguate

Freeform uses explicit disambiguation. Touch-hold empty canvas starts box selection, but Apple Pencil defaults to drawing; if the user wants Pencil to select/scroll, Apple exposes a setting and a toolbar toggle ([Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-nz/guide/ipad/ipad0b4f22b0/ipados)).

Concepts uses role separation. Two fingers pan/zoom/rotate the canvas, tap-and-hold invokes selection, and finger action can be configured so finger selects or pans while stylus keeps drawing ([Concepts](https://concepts.app/en/manual/selection), [Settings](https://concepts.app/en/manual/settings)).

Goodnotes mostly avoids ambiguity by making selection a tool choice and navigation a mode choice. You pick Lasso when you want selection; you switch to Read Only when you want safe scrolling/navigation ([Goodnotes](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/10779390143247-Select-content-with-the-Lasso-Tool), [Read Only Mode](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/7452151771663-Read-Only-Mode)).

FigJam on iPad also uses explicit tool state. You cannot select while a drawing tool is active, and while drawing you pan with two fingers. Long-press enters multi-select if you are not in a drawing tool ([FigJam iPad](https://help.figma.com/hc/en-us/articles/4502073572247-FigJam-for-iPad)).

Milanote uses long-press-canvas drag for multi-select on iPad and space-drag for pan on desktop. Because Milanote is less pen-centric, the conflict is simpler than in sketching apps ([Milanote](https://help.milanote.com/en/articles/111393-keyboard-shortcuts)).

## Recommendations for Pin & Paper

**Desktop v1.** Use classic canvas semantics: click selects one card/object, `Shift`-click adds or removes items, drag on empty canvas creates a rectangle marquee, `Esc` clears selection, and `Space` temporarily pans. This is the highest-confidence pattern from Figma and Milanote, and it will feel natural on Linux desktop.

**Touch/stylus v1.** Do not require a permanent select mode for normal use. Use tap to select one item, drag selected item to move it, and long-press on empty canvas to start a rectangular marquee. Once a multi-selection exists, keep the selection active and let direct taps on items toggle membership until the user taps empty canvas or presses a visible `Done`. Also allow two-finger pan/zoom at all times, including while a selection is active. This follows the strongest touch patterns from Freeform, FigJam, and Concepts without cluttering the UI.

**Group/ungroup model.** Make groups lightweight, explicit, and reversible. A group should keep each card editable; it should not flatten content. Single tap selects the group as a unit. A second tap, double-tap, or an `Edit Group` action should let the user target items inside it. Avoid Zinnia’s “ungroup repeatedly until you reach the thing you want” feel if you can; that model works for journaling assets but is friction-heavy for task cards.

**Z-order policy.** Use explicit z-order commands, not automatic “last touched goes on top.” New objects can appear on top when created, but selecting or dragging an existing object should not silently change its stacking order. For v1, `Bring to Front` and `Send to Back` are enough; stepwise `Bring Forward` / `Send Backward` can come later. This is the most stable policy across Zinnia, Freeform, Goodnotes, and Figma.

**Marquee semantics.** Prefer intersection-based rectangle selection for v1. Pin & Paper’s cards are large, spatial items; users will expect a marquee that catches touched cards, not only fully enclosed ones. If precision becomes a problem later, containment-only can be added as a modifier on desktop.

**One deliberate omission.** I would not copy Milanote’s “group into column” mental model. Pin & Paper’s spatial desk is closer to Freeform/Figma than to Milanote’s board-plus-container structure. Arbitrary groups are the better fit.

## Gaps I could not verify

I could not verify a current official Zinnia rectangle marquee, official additive/subtractive multi-select gestures, or a current Pixite-authored explanation of how selection interacts with Apple Pencil versus finger input inside the page editor.

I also could not verify a strong arbitrary group/ungroup object model in Milanote beyond its documented “Group into Column” action.
